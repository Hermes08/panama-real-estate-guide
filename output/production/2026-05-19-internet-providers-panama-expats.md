# Internet providers in Panama 2026: Tigo, +Móvil, Starlink (real prices by zone)

If you are moving USD $300k or more across borders to buy property in Panama, the internet question matters more than it sounds. Property closing, residency paperwork, and the first six months of remote work all depend on a connection that does not drop during a Zoom call with your attorney in Miami or your bank in Bogotá.

Three providers cover almost every realistic scenario in Panama in 2026: **Tigo Hogar** (the former Cable Onda, fiber and HFC), **+Móvil Hogar** (the state-affiliated symmetric-fiber challenger), and **Starlink** (the answer when fiber does not reach you). Below: real prices, what each provider actually does well, and how to pick by zone.

## The fiber providers (urban + suburban)

### Tigo Hogar (formerly Cable Onda)

Tigo is the largest fixed-line operator in Panama and the one most expats end up with by default, because it is what the building already has installed when you move in. Plans run 75 / 150 / 300 / 500 / 750 / 1000 Mbps. The 1000 Mbps tier requires actual fiber coverage at your address; the lower tiers are often delivered over HFC (cable), not fiber, which means real-world performance lags the advertised number during peak hours in dense buildings.

The upload number matters and Tigo does not lead with it: 300 / 500 / 750 plans all cap at **15 Mbps upload**. That is fine for streaming and most video calls, but if you are uploading drone footage of a finca for a buyer in Bogotá, or backing up a 30 GB folder to Dropbox, you will feel it.

Where Tigo wins: cable TV bundles (Tigo Sports has all 104 matches of the 2026 FIFA World Cup), Disney+ and ViX Premium included on the 500/750 plans for 12 months, and combo deals like FULL TIGO TODO INCLUIDO at B/.59/mo (500 Mbps internet + cable TV + a postpago mobile line). For a US retiree who wants one bill for everything, this is the path of least resistance.

Where to buy: [tigo.com.pa/internet/paquetes](https://www.tigo.com.pa/internet/paquetes).

### +Móvil Hogar (Más Móvil Fibra Fast)

The interesting challenger. +Móvil pushes **symmetric fiber**, meaning a 600 Mbps plan gives you 600 Mbps down AND 600 Mbps up. For remote workers, Zoom-heavy real estate brokers, or anyone uploading video content, symmetric upload is a category-defining difference.

Pricing as of May 2026:
- 300 Mbps fiber from B/.33/mo
- 300 Mbps + Universal+ TV: B/.53/mo
- 600 Mbps + Universal+: B/.56/mo
- 1000 Mbps available in covered zones

Free installation in 24 hours, advertised. Where it loses to Tigo: smaller national footprint, especially outside Panama City. Coverage is expanding but still not universal in coastal and highland markets.

Where to buy: [masmovilpanama.com/fibra-fast](https://www.masmovilpanama.com/fibra-fast).

### The HFC vs fiber distinction (matters)

Tigo's lower-tier plans (75-500 Mbps) often run over the old Cable Onda hybrid fiber-coaxial (HFC) network, which is shared bandwidth: your neighbors using Netflix at 8 pm will visibly slow your speed. +Móvil's Fibra Fast is fiber-to-the-home (FTTH), which is dedicated bandwidth: your link does not slow when your neighbors stream. If you are choosing between Tigo 500 HFC and +Móvil 300 fiber at similar prices, the +Móvil fiber will feel faster during peak hours despite the lower headline number.

## Starlink: the rural / remote answer

For Pedasí, Azuero coastline, agricultural fincas in Chiriquí, Bocas Bocas-side, anywhere the fiber trucks have not arrived, **Starlink is the answer**. Pricing (PA-specific, from starlink.com/pa/residential as of May 2026):

- **Residencial Lite**: USD $45/mo (unlimited data, lower priority)
- **Residencial**: USD $70/mo (best performance, up to 400+ Mbps)
- **Hardware (Starlink Kit)**: USD $200 one-time
- **Starlink Mini** for portable use: a separate kit, ~200 Mbps, fits in a backpack

What you get: 99.9% advertised uptime, 30-day trial, low-latency (good for video calls), unlimited data on both residential tiers. What you trade: a clear view of the sky (no overhanging trees or buildings blocking the antenna), a USD $200 upfront hit, and dependency on a single foreign-controlled provider for your home internet. The trade is worth it if your alternative is a 4G hotspot at 20 Mbps or no internet at all.

Where to buy: [starlink.com/pa/residential](https://www.starlink.com/pa/residential). Check coverage at [starlink.com/pa/map](https://www.starlink.com/pa/map).

## How to choose, in three questions

**1. Where is the property?**
If it is in central Panama City, Costa del Este, Punta Pacifica, or a managed building in Coronado / Boquete town center: pick between Tigo and +Móvil based on upload need. If it is rural, coastal-outside-town, or a finca: Starlink.

**2. Do you need symmetric upload?**
If yes (heavy video calls, content creator, broker uploading drone tours): +Móvil. If no (streaming, browsing, occasional Zoom): Tigo's bundles are cheaper effective dollars per service.

**3. What is your tolerance for one-time spend vs monthly?**
Tigo and +Móvil hide hardware cost in the plan. Starlink charges USD $200 upfront for the kit. If cash flow matters more than total cost, the fiber providers feel cheaper on month one. If total cost over two years matters more, the Starlink math is competitive even after the upfront hit.

## Common gotchas

**Asymmetric upload kills video calls.** Tigo's 15 Mbps cap on the lower-tier plans is fine until two people in the house are on simultaneous Zoom while a third uploads a photo album. If your work depends on video, pay for symmetric fiber (+Móvil) or accept Starlink.

**12-month promos expire.** Disney+ and ViX Premium on Tigo's 500/750 plans are included free for 12 months. After month 13, Disney+ adds B/.12.49/mo and ViX Premium adds B/.6.99/mo to your bill if you do not cancel them. Set a calendar reminder.

**Coverage maps oversell.** The "areas of cobertura" footnote on Tigo's 1000 Mbps plan is doing real work. Plenty of addresses that look covered on the provider map do not actually get fiber at the door; you get HFC instead. Verify in person before subscribing, or have the building admin confirm whether the building is fibered to the unit.

**ITBMS is 7%.** All advertised Panama internet prices exclude ITBMS (Panama's value-added tax). A B/.46 plan is actually B/.49.22 on the bill. Budget accordingly.

**Setup is fast or impossible.** Tigo and +Móvil quote 24-48 hours for new installations in covered zones. Outside their footprint, the answer is essentially "never." Do not assume a new build will get fiber until you have written confirmation from the provider.

**Power outages happen.** Most of urban Panama has stable electricity but coastal and highland areas (Bocas, Boquete, Pedasí) lose power for hours during the rainy season. A UPS for the router (~USD $80) is the cheapest single upgrade you can make.

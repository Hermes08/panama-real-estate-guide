## title

Bioma Costa del Este: Prices, Floor Plans, and What to Verify Before You Buy

## meta_description

Bioma is a preselling tower in Costa del Este from $342,000 for 65 m². Real per-unit prices, the actual delivery timeline (not the one some listings show), HOA costs, and what an attorney should check first.

## hook

Bioma doesn't have a unique legal angle the way Pino Alto has its Tourism Authority approval — there's no permit or title position here that no other Costa del Este tower can claim. What it actually competes on is scale: over 7,000 m² of amenity space split across three "ecosystems," including a street-level retail and sports-club podium (levels 0–4) with its own independent entrance so residents and the public don't share the same lobby, plus a track record from its developer, The Velopers, that's worth knowing about — their previous tower, Dovle in Coco del Mar, reportedly sold 170 units in 19 hours, which tells you more about resale liquidity than any marketing paragraph does. At roughly 60 storeys it also sits among the tallest towers in Costa del Este, alongside Paramount (63 storeys) and above older neighbors like Marea (38) and Top Towers (42). If the reason you're searching this project is "why this one and not the tower next door," the honest answer is amenity density and developer sales velocity, not a structural moat.

## body

**These numbers don't match every broker page, and you should know why before you get quoted a different figure.** Several resale and older listings (Encuentra24, IVASI, an earlier Panama Equity listing) show entry prices as low as $315,000–$335,000 for the same 65 m² unit. Business Panama Real Estate's listing shows "Sur"-facing units at meaningfully higher prices — $434,000–$489,000 for units in the 89–100 m² range, well above the "Model B/C/D" prices above. The pattern is consistent with what usually explains this kind of spread: earlier-phase or lower-floor units priced lower, later-release or better-oriented ("Sur") units priced higher, and some listings simply going stale after a price increase. Treat the table above as a starting point, not a guarantee, and get a current unit-specific quote before you commit.

Monthly HOA runs approximately $3.25–$3.50 per m², based on Panama Equity's published rate and an Encuentra24 listing showing $227.50/month on a 65 m² unit — call it roughly $210–$230 a month on the smallest units, scaling up from there. That's on the higher end for Costa del Este, which tracks with the amenity load.

### Where Bioma actually is

Bioma sits on Avenida Las Costas, directly across from Town Center mall — a real, walkable retail anchor, not a "nearby" claim that requires a car. Costa del Este itself is a planned neighborhood in the Juan Díaz township, built starting in the mid-1990s partly on reclaimed land: fill for the mangrove sections came from material excavated for the Panama Canal expansion and from a hill in nearby Villa Guadalupe that was leveled and trucked in (source: Wikipedia, citing the neighborhood's development history). This is true of essentially the entire district and every tower built on it, so it isn't a Bioma-specific red flag, but it's the kind of geotechnical fact worth having your engineer or attorney ask the developer about directly rather than assuming it's a non-issue because everyone else built here too.

The neighborhood connects to the rest of the city via the Corredor Sur highway and sits about 12 km from Tocumen International Airport. Costa del Este has its own central plaza, a roughly 4-kilometer boardwalk, and international schools including King's College Panama and Oxford International School — relevant if you're buying with kids in mind rather than as a pure rental play. For more on the area generally, see our [Costa del Este area page].

### What's included at Bioma

Translating the developer's amenity list out of Spanish marketing-speak and into what you'd actually walk into:

- **Ground-level podium (levels 0–4):** retail, restaurants, and a sports club open to both residents and the public, with a separate entrance and parking so the two don't mix
- **Pools:** three separate pools — described as "nature," "social," and children's — plus a jacuzzi
- **Indoor/outdoor entertainment:** an indoor cinema, a separate outdoor cinema, a bowling alley, an arcade/game room, a music studio, and a golf simulator
- **Family-specific spaces:** a kids' room, a baby's room, a playground, and a dedicated dog park
- **Work and wellness:** coworking space, a spa, and a fitness center
- **Food and social:** a sky bar/restaurant, a teppanyaki grill or chef's table, a library, and BBQ areas
- **Building infrastructure:** 10 elevators, a full backup electrical plant, and its own water reserve tank — worth noting given that water pressure and outages are a real, ongoing complaint in older Panama City towers

Total amenity space is cited at "over 7,000 m²" across the project — we could not independently verify the exact figure against a plan set, but it's consistent across the developer's own materials and multiple broker listings.

### Who Bioma suits — and who it doesn't

**It suits** buyers who want a heavily amenitized new-build in an already-established, walkable neighborhood rather than a raw investment gamble in an unproven area; investors comfortable holding a pre-construction position for several years before it converts to cash flow; and families who want international schools within a short drive and don't mind paying for a large shared-amenity building to get them.

**It doesn't suit** anyone who needs to move in within the next year or two — realistic delivery is years out, not months (see below). It doesn't suit buyers trying to minimize carrying costs: at $3.25+/m²/month, HOA here runs higher than older Costa del Este stock. And it doesn't suit anyone unwilling to do their own legal verification during the preselling phase — as of this research, we could not locate a public, independently verifiable construction permit or Registro Público filing tied specifically to this project, which is normal for a pre-construction tower but means you're relying on the developer's paperwork rather than a public record until units are titled.

### Before you buy at Bioma

**Status:** preselling / early construction. Excavation and site work at the location were documented as of early 2024 for a tower of this scale, which matters for the next point.

**Delivery date — this is where the public information genuinely disagrees, and we're not going to paper over it.** One listing aggregator states a Q4 2025 delivery — which, if taken literally, is already in the past as of this writing and implausible for a ~60-storey tower whose excavation was only underway in 2024. The Agency Panama's development page instead estimates delivery at 2028–2029, which lines up far better with the construction timeline and with how long comparable Panama City towers of this height have taken. **Treat the 2028–2029 estimate as the more credible one, and get the current delivery schedule in writing from the developer before you sign anything** — don't take either secondhand figure as confirmed.

**Deposit and payment structure**, per The Agency Panama's listing: reservation deposits start at $1,000, with down payments of 20% for Panamanian residents and 30% for foreign buyers, paid in installments during construction.

**Developer:** The Velopers, based in San Francisco, Panama City. Their portfolio includes Dovle (Coco del Mar), Velure (El Cangrejo), Vita, and Regalia (also in Costa del Este). We found sales-volume claims (the 170-units-in-19-hours figure for Dovle) but no independent reporting on whether The Velopers' prior projects delivered on their original timelines — that's worth asking references and past buyers directly, since we couldn't verify it from public sources.

**What an attorney should pull:** the *promesa de compraventa* (pre-construction purchase contract) and confirm its deposit-forfeiture terms; The Velopers' corporate registration; and, once available, the recorded construction permit and plano for the specific lot on Avenida Las Costas. None of this was independently verifiable through public sources during this research, which is expected at the preselling stage but is exactly the gap your attorney exists to close before you wire a deposit.

## faqs

1. **Q: How many floors does Bioma have?**
   A: Most sources — including the RE/MAX listing for the project and the most-cited site plans — put it at 60 storeys, but broker pages vary widely, from 47 to 67 floors depending on the source. Treat any single figure as approximate until it's confirmed against the recorded plans.

2. **Q: What's the starting price at Bioma?**
   A: Our data, aligned with the developer's own pricing, shows $342,000 for the smallest 65 m² unit. Some resale and older broker listings show units from this same floor plan priced as low as $315,000–$335,000, likely reflecting earlier-phase pricing or units that haven't been updated to current rates — confirm the live price before you commit.

3. **Q: When will Bioma be delivered?**
   A: Public sources disagree. One listing shows Q4 2025, which doesn't line up with a construction site that was still being excavated in early 2024 for a ~60-storey tower. A more detailed broker page (The Agency Panama) estimates 2028–2029, which we consider more credible — but get the developer's current schedule in writing rather than relying on either figure.

4. **Q: Who is the developer, and have they delivered projects before?**
   A: The Velopers, based in Panama City, with a portfolio that includes Dovle (Coco del Mar), Velure (El Cangrejo), Vita, and Regalia (also Costa del Este). Their Dovle project reportedly sold 170 units in 19 hours, which speaks to demand, but we could not independently verify their track record for delivering projects on the originally quoted timeline.

5. **Q: What are the HOA fees at Bioma?**
   A: Roughly $3.25–$3.50 per m² per month, which works out to about $210–$230/month on the smallest 65 m² unit and scales up with unit size — on the higher end for Costa del Este, consistent with the amenity load.

6. **Q: Is Bioma titled property?**
   A: Not yet. It's in the preselling/pre-construction stage, sold under a purchase promise (*promesa de compraventa*) rather than a titled deed. We could not locate a public, independently verifiable construction permit or Registro Público filing for this specific project during this research — normal at this stage, but something your attorney should confirm directly with the developer rather than assume.

7. **Q: How far is Bioma from the airport?**
   A: About 12 km from Tocumen International Airport via the Corredor Sur — a Costa del Este-wide figure, not unique to this tower.

# Boquete

## Identity

- **Region:** Chiriquí Highlands, western Panama, roughly 40 minutes by paved road from David (Panama's third-largest city) and about 300 miles / 6–7 hours by car from Panama City ([Travelmath](https://www.travelmath.com/driving-time/from/Panama+City,+Panama/to/Boquete,+Panama), checked July 2026).
- **Elevation:** the town itself sits around 3,900 ft (1,200 m) on the eastern flank of Volcán Barú, but the district ranges from roughly 1,000 ft to 11,398 ft at the volcano's summit ([Wikipedia: Boquete District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boquete_District); [International Living](https://internationalliving.com/countries/panama/boquete/), checked July 2026). That spread matters more here than in most areas — see Positioning below.
- **Climate:** "eternal spring" — highs in the high 70s to low 80s°F, lows rarely below 60°F at town-center elevation, with a dry season roughly mid-December to mid-April ([International Living](https://internationalliving.com/countries/panama/boquete/), checked July 2026).
- **Population:** 22,435 at the 2010 census across six corregimientos (Bajo Boquete, Alto Boquete, Caldera, Jaramillo, Los Naranjos, Palmira), with more recent expat-guide estimates putting the foreign-resident share at roughly one-fifth of the district ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boquete_District); [International Living](https://internationalliving.com/countries/panama/boquete/), checked July 2026). We could not find a post-2010 census figure from INEC (Panama's statistics institute) to update the base population number — treat it as dated.
- **Projects tracked on this site:** 3 — Pino Alto Boquete (preselling, $245,000–$395,000 per our data), Towncenter Boquete (delivered, $214,600–$625,000), and Boquete Country Club (delivered, unpublished pending data cleanup).
- **Price range:** among the two projects currently published on this site, **$214,600–$625,000**. A note on that number: our underlying dataset also carries an aggregate "from" price of $121,223 for the area, but that figure doesn't match any published listing and traces to a draft record (Boquete Country Club) with an internal data error — one of its unit models lists a size of 180,200 m², which is almost certainly a decimal-point typo. We're not publishing that project's numbers until it's cleaned up, and we're flagging rather than propagating the error.

## Positioning

Boquete's entire pitch is one number: it sits high enough on Volcán Barú that it stays spring-like while the rest of Panama is hot and humid at sea level. That's real, and it's why the town has one of the oldest, largest expat retirement communities in Central America.

What most listings don't say is that "no AC, no heating" is an elevation claim, not a town-wide one. Boquete's district spans roughly 1,000 ft to over 11,000 ft, and the difference matters: near downtown (around 3,400 ft), only about 1 in 20 luxury homes has air conditioning installed, but drop below 1,200–1,400 ft toward David and most homes need it; climb toward Volcancito or Jaramillo and evenings get cold enough that space heaters come out ([Casa Solution](https://casasolution.com/do-you-really-not-need-heating-or-air-conditioning-in-boquete-faq/), checked July 2026). A property described simply as "Boquete" could be either. Ask for the exact elevation, not just the town name.

## Is Boquete titled?

Mixed, and the answer depends on which parcel, not which town.

Panama's constitution bars foreigners (and any company with foreign capital, however small) from owning land within 10 kilometers of the Costa Rica or Colombia border (Constitución Política de la República de Panamá, Título X, Arts. 286 and 291). Boquete's town center is well clear of that line — one secondary source we checked (TheLatinvestor) states plainly that central Boquete is unaffected — but we have not independently verified the 10 km boundary against ANATI's (Autoridad Nacional de Administración de Tierras — Panama's national land authority) cadastral maps for any specific parcel, and Boquete's district runs west toward Volcán and the highlands, which sit closer to the frontier than the town center does. If a listing is on the western or northern edge of the district, get a survey that confirms its distance from the border before you do anything else.

Within that cleared zone, ownership is still not uniform. Formal developments in and around Bajo Boquete — the kind with a finca (a registered land parcel with its own file number at the Registro Público, Panama's public land registry) — are generally titled; that's the standard both Towncenter Boquete and Boquete Country Club are built on. Move toward the agricultural land and hillside lots outside town and Rights of Possession (derecho posesorio — an occupancy right recognized by ANATI but not registered at the Registro Público, and not mortgageable through a conventional bank) becomes common. Multiple sources researching foreign-buyer regret in Panama name exactly this pattern — retirees and remote workers falling for a Boquete property before checking whether it's titled or ROP — as one of the most frequently cited mistakes in the market (TheLatinvestor, checked July 2026). We have not personally pulled finca numbers for the three projects tracked on this page; until we have, ask the seller for the finca number and confirm it at the Registro Público before paying anything. See our [titled vs. Rights of Possession guide](/buying/titled-vs-rights-of-possession) for how to check.

## What it costs to live in Boquete

Estimates vary by source more than we'd like, which is itself worth saying plainly rather than picking the flattering number.

- **Rent:** a one-bedroom apartment near the town center runs roughly $650–$800/month, a furnished two-bedroom condo $139,000–$156,000 to buy, and a full house $600–$1,200/month to rent ([International Living](https://internationalliving.com/countries/panama/boquete/); [Wise cost-of-living](https://wise.com/us/cost-of-living/panama/boquete), checked July 2026).
- **Utilities:** roughly $58/month for electricity, water, and garbage on an 85 m² apartment at town-center elevation, mainly because most homes there skip AC and heating entirely ([Wise](https://wise.com/us/cost-of-living/panama/boquete), checked July 2026). International Living's own breakdown runs higher — $60–150 for electricity alone plus $0–25 for water/trash — so treat $58 as a floor for a small, efficient home rather than a typical bill.
- **Groceries:** $400–500/month for a couple ([International Living](https://internationalliving.com/countries/panama/boquete/), checked July 2026).
- **Whole-budget estimates disagree by roughly $1,000/month.** International Living puts a couple's full monthly budget at $1,403–$2,600; other expat-guide aggregations put it at $2,500–$3,500. We could not find a single authoritative figure — INEC does not publish a Boquete-specific cost-of-living index — so the honest range to plan around is **$1,500–$3,500/month for a couple**, depending on housing choice and lifestyle (checked July 2026).
- **What's not in that range:** the Pensionado program (Panama's pensioner-discount scheme) requires a verifiable pension of at least $1,000/month and, in exchange, gives 15–20% off hospital visits, 25% off restaurants, and 50% off entertainment ([International Living](https://internationalliving.com/countries/panama/boquete/), checked July 2026) — meaningful if you qualify, irrelevant if you don't.

## Who Boquete suits — and who it doesn't

**Suits:** retirees and remote workers who want a genuinely cool climate without leaving the tropics, who don't need a beach, and who are fine building a social life mostly among other expats — Boquete's foreign community is large, established, and functions comfortably in English. Fiber internet from +Móvil and Cable Onda reaches 200+ Mbps in parts of town, and a Selina coworking space runs about $10/day, so remote work is genuinely workable here (checked July 2026).

**Doesn't suit:**

- **Anyone who wants a beach lifestyle or quick access to one.** The nearest coastline is about an hour away.
- **Anyone who needs hospital-level care nearby.** There's no major hospital in Boquete itself; the two real options — Hospital Chiriquí and Mae Lewis Hospital — are both in David, about 40 minutes away.
- **Investors chasing liquidity or yield.** Gross rental yields run around 5–6%, and while a downtown Boquete property can sell in weeks, anything outside the town center can take a year or two to move ([The Wandering Investor](https://thewanderinginvestor.com/international-real-estate/the-pros-and-cons-of-investing-in-the-boquete-real-estate-market-in-panama/), checked July 2026).
- **Anyone allergic to rain.** The rainy season (roughly mid-April to mid-December) brings frequent afternoon drizzle locals call *bajareque*, and reader comments on more than one expat blog specifically flag landslide risk on rainy, mountainous roads and occasional break-ins as real (not hypothetical) concerns — the kind of thing promotional real-estate content tends to leave out (Vivatropical comment thread, checked July 2026).
- **Anyone relying on grid power without a backup plan.** Power outages during the rainy season are common enough that remote workers are routinely advised to keep a backup connection or battery on hand (The Wayward Road, checked July 2026).
- **Buyers who want to walk everywhere without a car.** That works in the town center. Outside it, mountain roads generally lack sidewalks, and owning a car becomes the practical default.

## Getting there and getting around

Fly into David's Enrique Malek International Airport (DAV) from Panama City's Albrook airport — Air Panama runs roughly two flights a day on that route, about an hour in the air (checked July 2026). From David, it's about 40 minutes / 26–30 miles to Boquete on a paved, four-lane road (Wikipedia; Travelmath, checked July 2026 — sources differ on the exact mileage by a few miles depending on the endpoint used). Driving the whole way from Panama City instead takes roughly 6–7 hours over about 300 miles, and estimates vary that much depending on the source and traffic ([Travelmath](https://www.travelmath.com/driving-time/from/Panama+City,+Panama/to/Boquete,+Panama), checked July 2026), which is why most buyers fly to David and drive the last leg.

Inside Boquete, the town center is walkable. Local buses ("colectivos") run between Boquete and David and stop around 9 p.m.; shared taxis cost roughly 50 cents to $1 depending on distance, private taxis $1.50–$5 (Expat Exchange; bestplacesintheworldtoretire.com, checked July 2026). Once you're outside downtown, a car stops being optional — the roads climbing into Alto Boquete, Jaramillo, and the hillside communities generally don't have sidewalks.

## FAQ

**Do I need air conditioning or heating in Boquete?**
Mostly not, if you're near the town center — average temperature there is around 68°F and it rarely drops below 60°F at night. But it's elevation-dependent: below roughly 1,200–1,400 ft (toward David) most homes do need AC, and above about 4,000 ft (Volcancito, Jaramillo) evenings get cold enough that people use space heaters. Ask the exact elevation of a specific property before assuming either way ([Casa Solution](https://casasolution.com/do-you-really-not-need-heating-or-air-conditioning-in-boquete-faq/), checked July 2026).

**Is land in Boquete titled or Rights of Possession?**
It depends on the parcel. Formal developments around Bajo Boquete are generally titled; agricultural and hillside land outside town is frequently sold as Rights of Possession. Ask for the finca number and verify it at the Registro Público before you pay anything — see the "Is Boquete titled?" section above.

**Can foreigners even buy property this close to the Costa Rica border?**
Panama's constitution bars foreign ownership within 10 km of the Costa Rica and Colombia borders (Título X, Arts. 286, 291). Central Boquete is generally understood to sit outside that zone, but we have not independently verified the boundary against ANATI's cadastral maps, and the western part of the district runs closer to the frontier. Get a survey for any parcel near the edge of town.

**How far is Boquete from an airport?**
About 40 minutes by car from David's Enrique Malek International Airport (DAV), which has roughly two Air Panama flights a day to and from Panama City (about an hour in the air). Driving the whole way from Panama City instead takes 6–7 hours (checked July 2026).

**Do I need a car in Boquete?**
Not if you live in the town center — it's walkable, and buses and taxis cover the rest. Outside downtown, yes: the mountain roads generally lack sidewalks.

**Is the internet good enough to work remotely from Boquete?**
Generally yes in town — fiber from +Móvil and Cable Onda reaches 200+ Mbps in parts of Boquete, and a Selina coworking space runs about $10/day. The caveat other remote workers raise is grid reliability: rainy-season power outages are common enough that a backup connection is worth having (checked July 2026).

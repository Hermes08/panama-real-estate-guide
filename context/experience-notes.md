## Origin / why developer-direct
In the international real estate market, the cleanest way to preserve the value of an investment is buying directly from the developer. You skip the resale margin, you get the developer's actual reservation pricing, and you have a counterparty with reputational stake in the project's completion. That is why panamarealestateguide.com positions developer-direct, not MLS aggregation: it is the structurally better deal for a buyer relocating capital across borders.

## Strong opinion the producer should defend
Most brokers in the Panama market run dual agency: they represent the seller and the buyer in the same transaction. That is a structural conflict of interest. The broker's incentive is to close, not to advocate. We operate as a buyer's agency: we represent only the buyer in any transaction we participate in. When a producer writes about "how to choose a broker in Panama" or any adjacent topic, this distinction should be made explicit (without naming competitors).

## Recurring customer situations the producer should know

### US clients
The decision is not about the apartment. It is about the lifestyle and the zone. Panama is a brand-new country for most US buyers. They need to get comfortable with the zone (climate, commute, hospital access, social scene, supermarket density) before any conversation about square meters or finishes makes sense. Articles aimed at US buyers should lead with zone fit, then narrow to inventory. Inverted from how we'd write for a local buyer.

### LATAM clients (Colombian buyers in particular)
Colombians are already accustomed to going back and forth to Panama. They know the country, the airport, the grocery prices. The educational lift is much lower. Articles aimed at Colombians can skip the "is Panama safe?" and "do I need a visa?" preamble and go straight to investment math, zoning, and rental yield.
